Home cooking curriculum · 6–12 months

Cooking is a control system.
Learn the variables, not the recipes.

You already run one precision hobby. This manual treats the kitchen the same way coffee treats extraction: a small set of variables — heat, moisture, time, salt, and structure — that you learn to read and tune. Recipes are just one set of coordinates. Once you understand the surface, you can navigate it yourself.

00

The system behind every dish

Almost every failure in a home kitchen is one of five variables drifting out of range. You don't memorise a hundred recipes; you learn to sense and control these five, then read the feedback the food gives you and correct. That loop — set, observe, correct — is the whole craft.

Why this framing works for you

A recipe hides the variables inside fixed numbers ("7 minutes"). That's fine until your pan, your stove, or your chicken is different — then it fails and you don't know why. Learn the variable ("cook to 74 °C internal, not to the clock") and every recipe becomes debuggable.

01

Four levels, every skill

Each module moves through the same four stages. Don't skip — L3 experiments only make sense once L2 is repeatable, and repetition at L2 only sticks once L1 is safe.

L1 · Functional — I can do it without failing or getting hurt L2 · Consistent — I reproduce a good result on demand L3 · Optimisation — I understand the variables and tune the outcome L4 · Mastery — I improvise, handle exceptions, and could teach it
02

The syllabus

Roughly ordered as a 6–12 month progression. Foundation and proteins first because they unblock everything else; fermentation and dough run long, so start their slow clocks early.

P0 · Kitchen Foundation

Knife skills & sharpening, mise en place, heat control. The precision base everything sits on. ~2 weeks

01 · Proteins

Chicken mastery (fix the dry-chicken problem) and advanced eggs. Cook protein without wrecking it. Month 1

02 · Rice, Beans & Grains

Your cheap, reliable meal base — white rice, fried rice, risotto, dried beans, gallo pinto. Month 2

03 · Sauces & Flavour

Where food turns "restaurant-like." Emulsions, reductions, pan sauces, salsas, stir-fry sauces. Month 3

04 · Vegetables

Roasting, high-heat, and fermenting — so vegetables become ingredients, not punishment. Month 4

05 · Bread & Dough

Gluten, hydration, fermentation timing, shaping. Pizza, focaccia, sandwich bread, sourdough. Month 5–6

06 · Fermentation

Your fermentation station: pickles, kraut, hot sauce, kimchi, and the road to miso. Month 6–7

07 · Meat & Fire

Steak, burgers, ribs. Direct vs indirect heat, resting, marinades, smoke. Host a barbecue. Month 7–9

08 · Restaurant Projects

Italian, Japanese, Mexican, French nights. Integrate everything into whole meals. Month 9–12

Priority order & weekly rhythm

If you optimise for your personality (precision hobby, likes experiments), attack in this order — it front-loads the skills with the highest transfer:

4Pizza dough05High reward, teaches dough deeply
5Sauces03Transforms everything else you make
6Grill skills07Social payoff, heat-control capstone
7Advanced baking05The long game once dough clicks

1 weekday session

Quick meal + deliberately practise one technique. E.g. chicken sandwich with a homemade sauce. Small, repeatable, low stakes.

1 weekend session

One longer project — a sourdough bake, a fermentation batch, a grill cook. This is where the slow-clock skills live.

The one rule that matters most

Start before you feel ready. Endless research is a way of avoiding the data. You learn cooking the way you learned dialling in a grinder: make it, measure it, note what drifted, change one thing, make it again. Open Phase 0 and cut an onion today.
