Doneness & carryover

Meat responds to internal temperature, not the number on the dial — and it keeps cooking for several minutes after you pull it off heat as residual heat migrates inward from the hotter outer layers. That rise is carryover: pull below your target by the carryover offset, and the center drifts up to meet it while it rests.

Safety — not negotiable Ground meat is contaminated throughout its mass by grinding, not just on the surface — searing the outside does not make the center safe. 71°C/160°F is a required minimum, not a target to hover near. Rare burgers are only safe if you grind whole-muscle meat fresh yourself and control the process.

Why carryover happens

The center of a piece of meat is always cooler than its surface while it's on heat; once removed, heat keeps conducting inward from the hot exterior toward the cooler core until they equalize. Bigger, thicker, denser cuts store more residual heat and have farther to conduct it, so they carry over more and for longer — a thick roast can climb 5°C/9°F or more, a thin cutlet barely 1–2°C/2–4°F.
