Aim for a final pH below 4.6 — that is what makes the ferment shelf-stable and safe. Keep everything submerged under the brine so the environment stays anaerobic. Cooler room temperature ferments slower but cleaner; warmer ferments faster but is riskier for off-flavours and unwanted microbes.

Safety notes

Fuzzy or coloured mould (green, black, pink, orange) means discard the batch. A thin, flat white film (kahm yeast) is usually harmless and can be skimmed off — but when in doubt, throw it out. For any canning or shelf-stable preserving beyond a simple refrigerator ferment, follow tested NCHFP guidance rather than improvising. Note also that garlic held in oil at room temperature is a distinct botulism risk — that is not the same situation as a salted, acidified ferment.
